A runner runs the named queues, each with how many of its jobs may run at
once, claiming only the kinds it has a handler for. It is made where the
program starts, started, and stopped as the program ends:

<Code code={runner} lang="ocaml" title="runner.ml" />

Nothing runs until `Runner.start`, which forks a fiber per queue, the
heartbeat and the listener on the switch it was made with.

## How it runs a job

| What | How |
|---|---|
| Claiming | one statement, `for update skip locked`, in a transaction that commits before the handler starts, so runners in several processes claim from one queue without waiting on each other |
| Leases | a claim holds a job until `lease_ends_at`; the heartbeat renews every lease the runner holds every quarter lease, and a runner that cannot renew for three quarters of it stops the job itself |
| Finishing | guarded by the runner and the attempt: a finish from a runner whose lease was taken changes nothing |
| Rescue | every runner's heartbeat makes every lapsed lease available again, its attempt counted, or discarded where it was the last |
| Backoff | 15 s doubled each attempt to at most six hours, spread by a tenth either way: twenty attempts span about two and a half days |
| Waking | `enqueue` notifies the table's channel with the queue's name; the poll is the truth and a notification a hint |
| Expiry | a job past `expires_at` is discarded unrun |
| Pruning | hourly, finished rows older than their retention: a day completed, a fortnight discarded or cancelled |
| Stopping | `stop` claims nothing more, waits a grace for running jobs, then puts back the rest with their attempt not counted |

A job is claimed, run and finished in three transactions, and the work is
in none of them, so a long job holds no connection and no row lock while it
runs.

## Its own connections

The runner's connections are its own -- a small pool and a listener --
because one borrowed from the application's would lose its heartbeats
exactly when the application is busiest, and a lost heartbeat is a job run
twice. It is given a `Rowtype_postgres.Conninfo.t`, read from a connection
string by `Rowtype_postgres.Conninfo.of_string`, and makes them itself.

## Its settings

| Argument | Unless given | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `~now` | | The clock every instant it writes or compares is read from |
| `?pool_size` | 2 | Its pool's connections |
| `?poll` | a second | How often a queue looks for due jobs between notifications |
| `?lease` | a minute | How long a claim holds without renewal, renewed every quarter of it |
| `?retain_completed` | a day | How long a completed row stays |
| `?retain_failed` | fourteen days | How long a discarded or cancelled row stays |
| `?observe` | none | A `Rowtype_postgres.observer` watching every statement on its pool, as an application's own |
| `~queues` | | Each queue it runs, with how many of its jobs may run at once |

Every wait is an `Mtime.Span.t` on the monotonic clock it is given, and
every instant the `now` it is given, never the database's clock.

## What it refuses

Before it answers, `Runner.create` checks every statement spool runs
against the database. It answers `Error` with what it refused, and
`Runner.refused_to_string` says it in words:

- `Unrun_queue`: a handler's kind is in no queue it runs.
- `Two_handlers`: one kind handled twice.
- `No_concurrency`: a queue given fewer than one slot.
- `Unreachable`: its pool could not be made.
- `Unmigrated`: the table is missing, or behind what spool runs -- spool's
  migrations are not applied, and each problem says where. Its pool is
  closed.

## Stopping

`Runner.stop` claims nothing more, waits up to its `?grace` (ten seconds)
for running jobs, puts back the rest, their attempt not counted, since they
were interrupted and did not fail, and closes its connections. It returns
within the grace and one statement.

A job is also stopped when its attempt passes its kind's timeout, when its
lease is lost, and when an operator cancels it. Each cancels the handler's
fiber at its next effect.

## Its log

The runner logs on the `Logs` source `spool`: each attempt and what it came
to, rescued and pruned rows, and a listener lost. No line carries a job's
payload.
