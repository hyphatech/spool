A handler is what one attempt at a job of a kind does. `Spool.handle` pairs
a kind with a function of the attempt and the payload, and the runner is
given the list:

## What it answers

A handler answers `Ok ()`, `Retry reason` (after the kind's backoff),
`Retry_in (span, reason)` (after what an upstream said) or `Discard reason`
(never again, and no attempt used: it cannot succeed however long it
waits). The reason is in words for an operator, kept with the attempt in
the job's `errors`.

A failed attempt that was the job's last discards it.

## A raise, and a timeout

A raise is a retry whose reason names the exception's constructor, never
its argument; the log line carries the rest. An attempt past its kind's
timeout is cancelled and is a failure.

A job's payload has no log level: no line the queue writes, at any level,
carries it.

## The attempt

`Spool.job` is the attempt a handler is running: its `id`, its `attempt`,
counting from one, its `max_attempts` and its `inserted_at`.

## A write with the completion

Where an attempt's work ends in a write to the application's own tables,
`Spool.handle_committing` makes it in the transaction that completes the
job. The handler does its work outside every transaction, as any does, and
answers the write:

```ocaml
Spool.handle_committing invoice (fun _job order ->
    let url = Pdf.render order in
    Ok (fun conn -> Invoices.set_pdf conn order url))
```

The job is completed and the write made together, or neither. The write
runs after the statement guarded by the lease, so a runner whose lease was
taken makes none. A write that answers a failure rolls both back and is
that failure -- retried, or discarded -- and one that raises is a retry
named by its constructor. Its connection is the runner's own, on the
database the runner was given, so the tables it writes are there.

## At least once

What a job runs is at least once and finished once. A write made with the
completion happens once; any other effect a handler has that must happen
once is idempotent, or keyed by the job -- as the handler above
gives the mail service the job's id, so a receipt sent twice is one.
